Research

My research is centered on theoretical and analytical problems in future wireless communication systems. Reconfigurable antennas are one part of this agenda rather than the identity of the overall research programme.

Wireless Communication Theory

I study mathematical models that explain how fading, geometry, interference, and receiver architecture determine reliability and achievable rate. A key objective is to obtain results with clear physical interpretation.

Channel Inference & Reconstruction

Full channel acquisition can be expensive. I therefore study how useful channel information can be recovered from partial observations, historical CSI, and known physical structure, including semi-blind and structured reconstruction approaches.

Distributed Wireless Networks

Distributed architectures such as cell-free networks introduce new interactions between local antenna behavior, interference, and network scalability. I study these effects through tractable analytical models.

Satellite & Non-Terrestrial Communications

Non-terrestrial systems impose different spatial and hardware constraints from terrestrial networks. My work in this area studies compact receive architectures, multi-user satellite links, and analytical design principles.

Reconfigurable Spatial Systems

Reconfigurable antenna concepts, including fluid antenna systems, appear in part of my work because they provide a useful platform for studying spatial degrees of freedom. They remain one component of a broader research agenda.
