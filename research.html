Research

My research agenda is broader than any single antenna concept. I am interested in how spatial structure, incomplete information, distributed operation, and non-terrestrial links reshape future wireless communication systems.

Wireless Communication Theory

A major part of my work develops mathematical models that explain how geometry, fading, interference, and receiver architecture determine reliability and achievable rate. The objective is not only to obtain formulas, but also to extract physical insight.

Channel Inference & Reconstruction

Full channel acquisition is often expensive. I therefore study how useful channel information can be inferred from partial observations, historical CSI, or known physical structure. This includes semi-blind strategies and structured reconstruction methods.

Distributed Wireless Networks

Distributed architectures such as cell-free systems create new interactions between local antenna behavior, interference, and scalability. I study how these effects can be modeled and exploited at the network level.

Satellite & Non-Terrestrial Communications

Non-terrestrial systems introduce different spatial and hardware constraints from terrestrial networks. My work in this area studies compact receive architectures, multi-user satellite links, and analytical design principles for future NTN systems.

Reconfigurable Spatial Systems

Reconfigurable antenna concepts, including fluid antenna systems, appear in part of my research because they provide a useful way to study spatial degrees of freedom. They are one technique within a wider research agenda rather than the identity of this website.
